# A 10 MW data hall on a conceptual-design mesh

*2026-09-18. Case `cases/hall-10mw.yaml`, run `runs/hall-10mw`, export
`results/hall-10mw`.*

The question this run answers is whether AICFD can validate a conceptual
design of a whole hall — not a POD — in a time an engineer will wait for, and
still say which rack is the worst. The answer is yes, with the resolution
trade stated plainly below.

## The design

Rows of 24 racks, 0,6 × 1,2 × 2,2 m, in pairs facing a contained hot aisle
1,2 m wide; a 1,8 m cold aisle between pairs; 1,8 m clear round every row; a
mechanical gallery 3 m deep along one long wall, and in the wall between them
one fan wall 4,5 × 4,0 m in front of every cold aisle. Sixteen PODs:

|  | value |
|---|---|
| racks | 768 (32 rows × 24) at 13 kW = 9,98 MW |
| hall | 18 × 88,2 m, 8 m to the slab, false ceiling at 6,5 m |
| fan walls | 17, one per cold aisle (15 between pods, 2 at the perimeter) |
| supply | 160 000 m³/h per unit, 2,72 M m³/h in all, at 20 °C |
| design ΔT | 11,0 K (rack airflow sized at 11 K, as for the POD) |
| return grilles | the whole ceiling over each hot aisle, K 2,4 (Koolair série 20.2) |

Two numbers in that table are choices, not data, and both are one line in the
YAML. Thirteen kilowatts a rack is what makes 16 PODs of 48 add up to 10 MW;
at 20 kW a rack the same hall is 10 PODs, and the same 17-per-5,4 m fan walls
would then need 5 m/s through their faces at 11 K — which is the first thing
a conceptual study of that variant would report. And 160 000 m³/h per unit is
what 768 racks ask for at 11 K divided by 17; the unit's own nominal flow from
its datasheet replaces it when transcribed.

## The mesh

One rack per cell along the row, two across, nine up:

|  | cell | why |
|---|---|---|
| x, along the row | 0,60 m | a rack is 0,6 m wide; the perimeter 1,8 m is 3 cells, the gallery 5 |
| y, across the rows | 0,30 m | racks 1,2 m, hot aisle 1,2 m, cold aisle 1,8 m and the 4,5 m fan wall all land on cell faces |
| z | 0,25 m | false ceiling 6,5 m, fan wall 4,0 m and slab 8,0 m exact; the rack is 9 cells tall so its top reads separately from its base |

35 × 294 × 32 = **329 280 cells**. The rack height snaps from 2,20 to 2,25 m
(the model says so in its warnings; nothing else moves). blockMesh, topoSet,
createBaffles and checkMesh take 11 s together and leave a valid mesh of 84
patches: 6 wall zones (all rack tops in one, all row ends in one, and so on),
17 fan pairs, 16 grille pairs.

The solver runs on 4 cores under MPI, the mesh cut into four slabs across y.
On the POD that is 3,6× faster than one core; here it is about 1,2 s per
iteration, and the sampler reconstructs each written time as it lands so the
run is watched the same way as a serial one.

## What was watched

Every hundred iterations: the mass and energy balances across all 17 fan
pairs, the return-path temperatures (hot aisles 1, 8 and 16; the plenum above
them; behind fan walls 1, 9 and 17), the rise across each unit, and every
rack's inlet.

## What this resolution can and cannot say

With a rack a single cell wide the model resolves rows, aisles and the
hall-scale pressure field; it does not resolve a rack's own recirculation or
the gap between two racks. So:

- the ranking of racks — which end of which row runs warmest, and by how much
  relative to the others — is what the run is for;
- a single rack's inlet carries 1 to 2 K of uncertainty, and the ASHRAE verdict
  per rack is indicative, not a sign-off;
- with every hot aisle contained, the inlet is set by the supply distribution
  and by leakage, both of which the coarse mesh captures; an uncontained
  variant would need finer cells across the aisle to trust the top-of-rack
  numbers.

The POD at 0,20 m cells remains the zoom: pick the worst POD from this run,
give it the pressures and temperatures the hall imposes, and resolve it there.
