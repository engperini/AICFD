# A 10 MW data hall on a conceptual-design mesh

*2026-09-18. Case `cases/hall-10mw.yaml`, run `runs/hall-10mw`, export
`results/hall-10mw`.*

The question this run answers is whether AICFD can validate a conceptual
design of a whole hall — not a POD — in a time an engineer will wait for, and
still say which rack is the worst. The answer is yes, with the resolution
trade stated plainly below.

## The design

Rows of 24 racks, 0,6 × 1,2 × 2,2 m, in pairs facing a contained hot aisle
1,2 m wide; a 1,8 m cold aisle between pairs; 1,8 m clear round every row; a
mechanical gallery 3 m deep along one long wall, and in the wall between them
one fan wall 4,5 × 4,0 m in front of every cold aisle. Sixteen PODs:

|  | value |
|---|---|
| racks | 768 (32 rows × 24) at 13 kW = 9,98 MW |
| hall | 18 × 88,2 m, 8 m to the slab, false ceiling at 6,5 m |
| fan walls | 17, one per cold aisle (15 between pods, 2 at the perimeter) |
| supply | 160 000 m³/h per unit, 2,72 M m³/h in all, at 20 °C |
| design ΔT | 11,0 K (rack airflow sized at 11 K, as for the POD) |
| return grilles | the whole ceiling over each hot aisle, K 2,4 (Koolair série 20.2) |

Two numbers in that table are choices, not data, and both are one line in the
YAML. Thirteen kilowatts a rack is what makes 16 PODs of 48 add up to 10 MW;
at 20 kW a rack the same hall is 10 PODs, and the same 17-per-5,4 m fan walls
would then need 5 m/s through their faces at 11 K — which is the first thing
a conceptual study of that variant would report. And 160 000 m³/h per unit is
what 768 racks ask for at 11 K divided by 17; the unit's own nominal flow from
its datasheet replaces it when transcribed.

## The mesh

One rack per cell along the row, two across, nine up:

|  | cell | why |
|---|---|---|
| x, along the row | 0,60 m | a rack is 0,6 m wide; the perimeter 1,8 m is 3 cells, the gallery 5 |
| y, across the rows | 0,30 m | racks 1,2 m, hot aisle 1,2 m, cold aisle 1,8 m and the 4,5 m fan wall all land on cell faces |
| z | 0,25 m | false ceiling 6,5 m, fan wall 4,0 m and slab 8,0 m exact; the rack is 9 cells tall so its top reads separately from its base |

35 × 294 × 32 = **329 280 cells**. The rack height snaps from 2,20 to 2,25 m
(the model says so in its warnings; nothing else moves). blockMesh, topoSet,
createBaffles and checkMesh take 11 s together and leave a valid mesh of 84
patches: 6 wall zones (all rack tops in one, all row ends in one, and so on),
17 fan pairs, 16 grille pairs.

The solver runs on 4 cores under MPI, the mesh cut into four slabs across y.
On the POD that is 3,6× faster than one core; here it is about 1,2 s per
iteration, and the sampler reconstructs each written time as it lands so the
run is watched the same way as a serial one.

## What was watched

Every hundred iterations: the mass and energy balances across all 17 fan
pairs, the return-path temperatures (hot aisles 1, 8 and 16; the plenum above
them; behind fan walls 1, 9 and 17), the rise across each unit, and every
rack's inlet.

## The result, stopped at iteration 674

The run was stopped by hand at 674 iterations (about 14 minutes on 4 cores):
the places had not moved more than 0,04 K per hundred iterations since 300,
and the design it was describing was not one worth converging further -- see
below. The export is the write at 600. **All eleven checks pass**:

```
Fan walls       17 x 160 000 m³/h (909,8 kg/s) at 20,0 °C
Return air      30,9 °C (dT 10,9 K)
Heat carried    9 978 kW of 9 984 kW installed (100%)
Return path     31,0 -> 30,6 -> 30,9 °C, 0,30 K apart

Fan wall rise   81,0 Pa of 100 on the most loaded unit (fan13), 77,0 on the least
Rack rows       27,1 Pa in the field, 24,8 Pa from the curve (109%; rows 26,6 to 29,3)
Return grilles  11,8 Pa in the field, 10,7 Pa from K (111%)

768 racks: inlet at the top 20,1 to 20,5 °C, none above ASHRAE recommended
```

Mass closes across seventeen pairs to five figures and the load comes back
in the return air to 0,06%. With every hot aisle contained, every rack
breathes supply air: the warmest inlet in the hall is 0,5 K above the fan
discharge, at positions 5 to 8 of the two outermost rows. That part of the
concept works.

Two things do not, and the run is worth keeping for them.

**The first rack of every row is starved.** Along the cold aisle the racks
pass 100 to 118% of their rated airflow -- except position 1, nearest the
fan wall, which passes 22 to 38%. A 4,5 m unit discharges into a 1,8 m
aisle; the air accelerates to about 5 m/s at the aisle mouth and the static
pressure there is 61 Pa against 78 Pa a few metres in. The rack at the mouth
sits in that 17 Pa trough. Modelled as a resistance (ADR-011) it passes what
the pressure gives it, and its outlet reaches 62 °C -- the peak that stretches
the temperature colour scale. Real rack fans would draw the air; the trough is
real all the same, and 1,8 m of clearance is not enough room for a 4,5 m
discharge to slow down before the first rack.

**The plenum is the bottleneck.** Along the loop, referred to the intake:
cold aisle 78 Pa, hot aisle 46, plenum over the rows 26, plenum at the
dividing wall -3, gallery -3,5. Racks 27 Pa, grilles 12, and about 30 Pa for
2,7 million m³/h to travel the 1,5 m plenum and pass the opening above the
wall at 6 m/s (9,5 m/s peak just past it). In the POD the same leg cost one
pascal. Eighty pascals of a hundred is a fan wall with no margin for filters
ageing or a unit out of service.

Both are one-line changes to try: `aisles.perimeter` at 3 m or more in front
of the rows, and a plenum of 2,5 m (slab at 9 m, or ceiling at 5,5 m). Neither
was run; the user is taking the hall to another tool for comparison first.

## Second run: the plant as its datasheet gives it

The first run's fan walls were invented to close the loop: 17 units of
160 000 m³/h, one per cold aisle, at an 11 K rack rise. The real selection is
a Vertiv CWA CA40NPVG6 chosen for QR2: 76 650 m³/h and 290,9 kW sensible per
unit, 22,1 °C supply against a 37 °C return, ESP 100 Pa, 2,23 × 3,67 m — and
selected at 1 880 m, where the sheet itself says the same unit moves 56 475
m³/h of *standard* air. Sized by the office's rules (ADR-023) the hall needs
**35 units**: 34 fail both criteria, 35 gives 102% of the load in kilowatts
and 100% of the racks' airflow at 158 CFM/kW. The case now carries those
numbers, the units spread evenly along the gallery wall, and the solver runs
at the site's 80,7 kPa (air at 0,952 kg/m³).

Stopped at 541 iterations, exported at 500, settled to 0,03 K per hundred:

```
Fan walls       35 x 76 650 m³/h (709,5 kg/s) at 22,1 °C
Return air      36,1 °C (dT 14,0 K)               -- the datasheet's 37 °C selection
Heat carried    9 983 kW of 9 984 kW (100%)
Fan wall rise   73,2 Pa of 100 on the most loaded unit, 67,5 on the least
Rack rows       26,8 Pa in the field, 25,0 from the curve (107%)
Return grilles  9,2 Pa in the field, 8,3 from K (111%)
768 racks       inlet at the top 22,2 to 22,8 °C, none above recommended
```

Along the loop, referred to the intake: cold aisles 66 Pa, hot aisles 40,
plenum over the rows 24, plenum at the wall 1, gallery −2. The plenum leg
fell from 30 to 23 Pa — the same volume now moves through 35 openings instead
of 17, and the return over the wall slowed from 6,3 to 5,6 m/s — but it is
still the largest single cost after the racks. The first rack of every row
still passes 21 to 24% of its rated airflow: a continuous fan wall does not
remove the trough at the aisle mouth (55 Pa against 68 a few metres in),
because it is the perimeter corridor turning into a 1,8 m aisle that makes
it, not the unit spacing. That one is geometry.

The rack inlets rose 2 K with the supply (22,1 against 20 °C) and the return
rose 5 K with the altitude and the 158 CFM/kW rule. Both are what the
datasheet says they should be; the concept's margin against ASHRAE's 27 °C
went from 6,5 K to 4,2 K.

## What this resolution can and cannot say

With a rack a single cell wide the model resolves rows, aisles and the
hall-scale pressure field; it does not resolve a rack's own recirculation or
the gap between two racks. So:

- the ranking of racks — which end of which row runs warmest, and by how much
  relative to the others — is what the run is for;
- a single rack's inlet carries 1 to 2 K of uncertainty, and the ASHRAE verdict
  per rack is indicative, not a sign-off;
- with every hot aisle contained, the inlet is set by the supply distribution
  and by leakage, both of which the coarse mesh captures; an uncontained
  variant would need finer cells across the aisle to trust the top-of-rack
  numbers.

The POD at 0,20 m cells remains the zoom: pick the worst POD from this run,
give it the pressures and temperatures the hall imposes, and resolve it there.
